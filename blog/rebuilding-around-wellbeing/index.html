Rebuilding Around Wellbeing

October 23, 2026 — a quick journal entry

The flip

I think inspiration and the forms of success I'm trying to achieve come from becoming a person who makes exciting opportunities happen. Going to events, experiencing culture, speaking to strangers, organising meetups and participating in groups.

My fitness, financial and social goals only become achievable once I embrace my hobbies and pursue my goals.

I used to only think of my skills, health/fitness and finances as the things I need to be building. But without building a beautiful life, life is a bit meaningless. Rather than building life around those other ideas, this time I'm going to try rebuilding around my wellbeing. Fitness so I can enjoy running club and dancing. Money so I can say yes to gelato and events. Skills so I can do work I find meaningful. Each one matters to the extent that it makes the life richer.

Baby steps

My new mantra is "baby steps". I only commit to a small, limited-scope version of my goal — something you could do in 20 or 40 minutes. I do this because it builds momentum and protects my energy.

Along with baby steps, I'm teaching myself to indulge in a way that is detached from guilt:

  • Eating beautiful authentic gelato and savouring it without thinking about money or calories.
  • Spending an evening lounging in the spa after a gym session.
  • Going to the park and doing nothing for 2 hours. Just enjoying seeing the people and really savouring my food.

The gelato, the spa and the park aren't rewards or recovery. They're the life I'm building. And as a side effect — not the reason — the other goals get easier once I'm actually enjoying my hobbies.

What would rebuilding around wellbeing look like?

  • Review the Science of Wellbeing course material. I once completed it and learnt techniques like really exploring your interests and doing fun adventurous things. But I haven't really considered my wellbeing as something I should be spending a lot of time and energy to cultivate. I'll complete the course again while I rebuild — one module per sitting, then pick a single technique to try each week.
  • Pursue lots of interesting events and build new friendships.
  • Talk to strangers. Start with one brief exchange per outing, and follow up with a curious question.

The bottleneck is me, not access

I recently watched the movie "I Love You, Man" and it really resonated with me. I have all these amazing friends but it often feels like I need more — and I don't know how. My friendships have always come slowly, and mostly through structured settings like work and school.

Some simple easy things I've been neglecting:

  • Going to the office for work
  • Bachata classes
  • Running club

But actually the main issue with these hasn't been the activity — it's been me. I've had plenty of opportunities, I just haven't been putting myself out there to make the friends. I don't need more opportunities, just a small, repeatable habit of reaching out within the ones I already have: set a tiny goal per session like learning one person's name and one thing about them, and ask someone to grab a coffee after.

Tonight's test run

I'm playing Magic tonight, which I love. I should consider how I interact with people and try to engage on a more personal level — learn one personal detail about someone beyond the cards, and ask if they want to play again or join me at another event.
